Reaching aiden-studio

The brewer dashboard runs as a service on the Mac mini and listens only on that machine. Here is every way to open it, from the Mac mini, from any of your computers through Tailscale, and the SSH fallback.

Addresses

  • Any of your computers, with Tailscale https://cschwedas-mac-mini.taildc8082.ts.net Works from anywhere on any device signed in to your Tailscale account. No port, real certificate. Nothing else can reach it. Use this full name exactly: the short name and the two IP addresses the Tailscale console also lists do connect, but the certificate is issued for the full name only, so a browser warns on any of them.
  • On the Mac mini http://localhost:5150 Always works. Bookmarked in Chrome.
  • On the Mac mini, by name http://aiden.local:5150 Needs the two lines in /etc/hosts (127.0.0.1 aiden.local and ::1 aiden.local). With only the first line every page load waits five seconds for Bonjour. The port is part of the address; port 80 would need root.
  • On the Mac mini, no setup http://aiden.localhost:5150 macOS resolves every *.localhost name to itself, instantly.
  • On the laptop, through the tunnel http://localhost:5150 Only while the SSH command below is running. The aiden.local alias exists on the Mac mini alone, so on the laptop it is always localhost.

With Tailscale, from any of your computers

Tailscale joins your computers into a private network only they can see. The Mac mini publishes the dashboard onto it; the app itself still listens only on the Mac mini.

  1. Already done on the Mac mini: Tailscale installed and signed in as cschweda@github, the dashboard published with tailscale serve --bg 5150, and the app told to answer to its network name. Two admin-console settings keep it that way: DNS, HTTPS certificates on; Machines, the Mac mini, key expiry disabled.
  2. On each other computer: install Tailscale (Mac: App Store or tailscale.com; Windows: the installer from tailscale.com) and sign in with the same account. That is the whole setup.
  3. Open https://cschwedas-mac-mini.taildc8082.ts.net in any browser on that computer.

Only the login cschweda@github may make changes (start a brew, edit profiles or schedules, mark descaled); any other member of the network can only look. Never run tailscale funnel: that publishes the address to the whole internet, and the app has no login of its own.

Without Tailscale: the SSH tunnel from the laptop

The app has no login screen, so it never listens beyond the Mac mini itself. An SSH tunnel carries the laptop's localhost:5150 to the Mac mini's, and SSH asks for the Mac's own password, which is the login the dashboard would otherwise lack.

  1. Once, on the Mac mini: System Settings, General, Sharing, turn on Remote Login. Leave it on.
  2. Each time, on the laptop (Terminal on a Mac, PowerShell on Windows, both have ssh): 
    ssh -N -L 5150:127.0.0.1:5150 cschweda@cschwedas-Mac-mini.local
    Enter the Mac mini's account password. The command prints nothing and keeps running; that is the tunnel.
  3. Open http://localhost:5150 in the laptop's browser.
  4. To finish, press Ctrl+C in that terminal. The dashboard on the Mac mini is unaffected.

If the name does not resolve on the laptop, use the Mac mini's Wi-Fi address instead, which System Settings, Wi-Fi, Details shows, or this command on the Mac mini prints:

ipconfig getifaddr en0

Then ssh -N -L 5150:127.0.0.1:5150 cschweda@192.168.x.x with that address.

A tunnel left open for hours can time out on some routers. Adding -o ServerAliveInterval=60 to the command keeps it alive.

What reaches it and what doesn't

DeviceReaches it?Why
The Mac miniYesThe service listens on 127.0.0.1:5150.
The laptop, with the tunnelYesSSH carries loopback across, behind the Mac's login.
Any computer or phone signed in to your Tailscale accountYesTailscale carries it privately; from home or anywhere else.
The laptop, without Tailscale or the tunnelNoNothing listens on the Wi-Fi interface, by design.
Anyone not signed in to your accountNoA public address would need the app's own login first; that is Phase 2.

Do not change HOST in .env or server.host in aiden.config.ts to reach it from elsewhere: the startup guard refuses any address other than loopback while there is no login, on purpose. The brewer would otherwise be controllable by anyone on the network.

Checking on the service, from the Mac mini

All from the repo at /Volumes/satechi/webdev/aiden-portal.

CommandWhat it does
deploy/local/status.shLoaded? Running? Answering? The last log lines.
deploy/local/logs.shFollows the app's log. Ctrl+C to stop.
pnpm build && deploy/local/install.shAfter any code, config, or .env change. Restarts the service.
deploy/local/uninstall.shStops it. Add --purge to remove the installed copy, logs, and brew history too.
curl -s localhost:5150/api/status | jqVersion, dry run, and whether Fellow answered.

While the service is installed it holds port 5150, so a dev server needs another port: PORT=5151 pnpm dev.
